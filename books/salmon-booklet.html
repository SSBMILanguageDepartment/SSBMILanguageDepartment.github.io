Mayé Wadaahá (A Salmon's Life)

The lifecycle of Chinook salmon in the Sacramento River

By the SSBMI Environmental Department and SSBMI Language Department

About

Séekas’in! (Hello!) Thank you for exploring our booklet about may (Chinook salmon) in the Sacramento River.

This booklet is part of a joint project between the SSBMI Environmental Department and the SSBMI Language Department in 2026 focusing on salmon, non-point source pollution, and their impacts on our community. The booklet also introduces vocabulary in the Nisenáan (Nisenan) language for the life stages of Chinook salmon.

We produced a limited run of full-color booklets and a sticker sheet highlighting the life stages of salmon; you can contact language@ssband.org to check on their availability. You can also access the following resources by clicking on the tabs above:

  • An ebook version of the full-color booklet with audio narration
  • A downloadable, print-at-home coloring book version of the booklet
  • Audio recordings of the Chinook salmon life stages sticker set
  • A list of key vocabulary with audio recordings

    • The Nisenáan Language

    The SSBMI Community has ties to the Valley and Southern Hill dialects of the Nisenan language through the Tribe’s Matriarchs, Pamela Cleanso Adams and Annie Hill Murray Paris. Pamela spoke Valley Nisenan. Based on where she was from and the language of her relatives, Annie spoke Southern Hill Nisenan.

    The map below shows these dialect regions and neighboring dialect and language regions.

    Map of the dialect areas of the Nisenáan language adapted from the book "California Indian languages" by Victor Golla (2011). If you would like more information or have questions, please contact us at language@ssband.org.

    The SSBMI Community is revitalizing the Nisenan language through historical records that our Nisenan Ancestors left for us. We do not have Nisenan language equivalents for "alevin", "fry", and "smolt" in these historical records; the SSBMI Language Department used Nisenan words and word-formation strategies to create new terms.

    Our Language FAQ pamphlet has more information about the SSBMI Community’s language revitalization work.

    The Environmental Department’s brochure has more information about their environmental restoration work.
